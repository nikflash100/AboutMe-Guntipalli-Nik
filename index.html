About

I'm in seventh grade in Minnesota. I write Python, mostly machine learning.

Most learning apps do the same thing. They dump a pile of random material on you and hope one piece of it helps. That's backwards. You don't need more material, you need someone to point at the one thing you're missing. Every project I've built is some version of finding that thing.

Outside of code I do quizbowl, chess, and Destination Imagination. My DI team won the Minnesota state championship in the Middle School Science Division in 2026, and I built the electronics and timer system for it.

Projects

Signal

Adaptive diagnostic app for K–12

Signal diagnoses a student instead of guessing. It doesn't throw a pile of random courses at you, it works out what you actually need and gives you that.

It opens with a 16-question screen across a bunch of topics, then narrows with a staircase algorithm and a re-check pass until it lands on one weak spot. Five model calls end to end.

Most of the work went into making it not break. There's a 40-question offline bank for when the API is down, state that survives a force-close, streak tracking, and routing by grade band.

Thunkable · OpenAI gpt-4o-mini · Shown at the Spark Civics Civic Innovation Showcase

SuccessForecast

Predicting student outcomes

A logistic regression model that predicts whether a student passes. 85.6% accuracy, 0.92 ROC-AUC. I ran K-Means on the same data and got a handful of clear student archetypes out of it, which I wasn't expecting. Presented it at demo day with the notebook and a slide deck.

Python · scikit-learn · pandas · matplotlib

Buzzer Station

Quizbowl practice app

The parser was the real project. I pulled 3,531 questions out of public packet archives, 1,885 tossups and 1,646 bonuses, and got them into a format I could actually use.

The app on top of it reads questions word by word, handles power markers and spacebar buzz-in, grades answers, scores by category, and re-drills whatever I missed.
